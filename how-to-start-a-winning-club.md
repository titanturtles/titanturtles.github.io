# How to start a winning club? 

I don’t really know a good answer to the question. But, I can show you what we did and what our thought processes were. (Or, at least, I can show you what I want to show.) If that helps.


## *Give up*

>*“... should he strengthen his left, he will weaken his right; should he strengthen his right, he will weaken his left. If he sends reinforcements everywhere, he will be weak everywhere.” – Sun Tzu*

We were introduced to the CyberPatriot competition in summer 2020 when one of our friends formed a team and invited our kids to play in the competition. It was CyberPatriot XIII. Our kids loved it even though the whole team knew very little about cybersecurity. But, they loved it.  

At that time, my wife Pei and I were actively coaching a First Lego League (FLL) robotics team. The team did pretty well, we won the Oregon Champion that year. But, because of the COVID-19 pandemic, coaching a robotics team is not that easy. There wasn’t a world festival hosted at Houston that year, either. And, at that time, we don’t know when the pandemic will end. Plus, our kids spent an unhealthy amount of time in the robotics team given that our elder son was also in an FTC team. So, our entire family had some serious conversation. And, we decided to quit robotics completely and to play the CyberPatriot competition even though we knew literally nothing about cybersecurity besides I had a week long cybersecurity training. That’s all I knew about cybersecurity. It was not an easy choice at least for me. But, we knew we had to give up robotics if we choose to do cyber, otherwise, we will fail on both.

Over the years, I saw a lot of people try to participate in a lot of things, maths, programming, robotics, science bowls, debate, chess, soccer, swimming, and etc.. And they failed. My suggestion is, try to only focus on a few things, make sure you hold yourself accountable on things that you choose to do. **Give up means focus!**


## *To start a team*

Actually, initially, we wanted to go the easy route to let our kids join another team managed by a very good coach. But, then, that team only had one open spot. Can’t fit both our kids there. So, we decided to coach the team ourselves.

People asked me to write a blog post about “how to start a winning club”. Well, technically, I don’t really know how to start a winning club. To win, especially to win the championship, you need some luck. But, at least, we thought we knew how to start a team. Registration was not hard. At that time, I was the chairperson of our kids’ high school PTO. So, Pei and I didn’t have too much trouble becoming coaches for high school teams. Pei and I learned one thing from being FLL coaches – read the freaking documents, period. So, we read through the entire uscyberpatriot.org website. We googled everything we could about CyberPatriot. (Talking about google, a lot of people stop on page 2. I sometimes google up to the hundredth page.) And, we realized that there are some legendary teams and legendary coaches. For some reason, we also learned that there is a CyberPatriot discord server. So, we joined the CyberPatriot discord server immediately. Within a month, we got super valuable advice from both Coach Allen and Coach Paul. Literally, we took their advice word by word. We let students teach. We design our own turtle dollars. We put their quotes on our turtle dollars:

>*“Compete all you can compete, read all you can read, learn something new every time you compete, always compete with someone better than you!”*

As seasoned FLL coaches, we thought we knew how to start a team. We just invited some kids we knew to join the team. All we needed was 6 students. But, then, during the short period prior to round 1 of the CyberPatriot competition, 4 kids quit. They didn’t quit at the same time, but we had to find some new students every time someone quit. And, all these students who quit are straight A students and went to top colleges (I mean H) later on. When we looked back, we realized we were not reputable or reliable at all. Luckily, most of the students who didn’t quit in year one, eventually qualified for the CyberPatriot national final with us.

The situation 6 years ago was not the same as today. During that time, we don’t really know what to learn or what to do. So, every night, I went through the historical messages in the CyberPatriot discord server to figure out what we need to study to improve. My mindset is if someone else can figure it out, we can figure it out as well. Then, I found out that a user there is quite knowledgeable and has a very good methodology to inspire students to do the research and study themselves. So, luckily, I cold called the user to see if he is willing to be our mentor and luckily, he said yes. Which later on to be proved as my best decision. Mentor Sean, or tirefire on discord, is a professional cybersecurity engineer. The more advanced our students are, the more valuable he is to those students. And, he usually doesn't give the answer directly when the students ask him questions. As a matter of fact, when students ask him questions, a lot of times, he asks more questions back. So, the students have to do some real meaningful research to get the answers. That turned out to have better results than directly answering the questions. And, I myself learned a lot from Mentor Sean.

So, to start a team, you really need to find people, not only find students, but also find people who can give you some guidance. Reaching out to people and listening to people’s advice is a very easy way to get an edge when you start something new you are not familiar with. Don’t be shy. The worst you can get is if they say “No”. And, over the years, I learned one thing. In any industry or in any field, the best people usually have a forum or a network circle, in that forum or circle, they are willing to share high quality knowledge. They improve together. Join that circle as soon as you can.


## *Build a club*

If you only have one 6-student team, it’s not easy to form a winning team. Because, usually, not all the team members will put enough effort in it. And, over the years, I learned that hard-working itself is a flair, is a gift. At least that’s the case in our club. At the time when we started, CyberAegis had 120 students and 20 teams participating in CyberPatriot. Troy High had over 300 students and over 50 teams participating in CyberPatriot. Half Dome also had at least 10 teams. And, if you do some research, you will find that nearly all national finalist teams for CyberPatriot are from clubs with multiple teams. This is the same in all sports. In order to win, you need to have some Messi, Ronaldo (R9 or prime CR7) and Haaland in your team. And, you need a little bit of a bigger base to find those people. In CyberPatriot, fortunately, we don’t really need Messi or Ronaldo to be able to win. But, at least we need students who can really put in enough effort in your team 1 or varsity team.

And, for that reason, we expanded our team to a club with multiple teams the 2nd year we were coaching cybersecurity. We designed the posters. We designed a tri-fold board. In the club rush, we bought candies and chocolates to try to attract students. And, school figured out what we did and banned candies and food during the club rush the next year. We asked all our team members to persuade their friends to join the club.  Some new students are coming from our team members' soccer team, math club, robotic club, programming club, tennis team, swimming team and even Deca team. I think we did our best to recruit students that year.

I sometimes joke about how to advance to the semifinal and advance to the national final in the CyberPatriot competition. I said *“it’s very simple, in order to advance to the semifinal in the HS open division, you need to be in the top 7.5% of the teams. So, your whole team needs to work harder than 92.5% of the teams cumulatively. And, if you want to advance to the national final, you need to work harder than 99.8% of the teams cumulatively. And, ‘work hard’ includes the recruiting part as well...”*

So, if you have the ambition, build a club!


## *Let students do it*

When I read Feynman’s Surely You’re Joking, I learned that if you can explain some complex subject in a very clear and simple way, you fully understand it. (Some may argue with me that yes they understand it, it is just hard to explain it. I would say if that’s the case it means that you just learned it, but your brain has not optimized the way to store or to load the knowledge. Once the knowledge storage is optimized, it is easy to use and you will never forget it.) Otherwise, you yourself don’t really fully understand it, yet. And, from both Coach Allen and Coach Paul we learned that they also let students teach. So, we know, we need to let students teach, period.

It’s not easy. Of course it’s not easy. Think about it, for example, you are new to a chess club, you just learned how to play chess. And, the teacher said, *“Ok, everyone, pick a research topic, present it to the club next time. You, Adam, can learn the rook endgame and present it next time. Ethan, you can present the pawn endgame. And, hey, Akshay, you can present the queen vs pawn endgame next week. Alright, we are dismissed …”* Or, you are in a soccer team, and the coach said, *“Hey, Zlatan, learn Neymar's dribbling skill, and teach us the technique next Wednesday …”* Quite a few students won’t feel very comfortable even sitting in the room. 

We are lucky enough that some students were able to step out of their comfort zones when we started. Very soon, a handful of students started to sign up for some topics, studied them, prepared the slides, and were brave enough to stand in front of the club to present and demo what they have learned. Maybe their presentation is not good enough. Maybe the demo is not state of the art. Maybe, a lot of the time, those students who presented did not fully understand the topics and whatever they presented were incorrect. But, at least for those students who presented, they actually spent time looking for tutorials of the topic, looking for demonstrations of the topic. At least, maybe for the sake of not losing face in front of the club, they themselves started to really try to understand it and started to test what’s going on. As a coach, do I really know all of the topics? No. And, I doubt anyone will know everything needed for CyberPatriot on top of their heads. If we coaches or mentors don’t know everything, we should not limit our students’ knowledge. We always talk about how your students need to be better than you. We really mean it. Gradually, they started to understand what are the things worth learning; what is the best way to learn those things. The most important thing is starting from now on, some of the students are really motivated. And, they can improve themselves without much help from the coaches. Coaches can now focus more on other administrative things.

We not only let students teach, but also let students create practice images (virtual machines) including the team assignment images. At the end, the students learned a lot by just creating those images (we will elaborate more later). Also, when you have done so much for the club, you feel the ownership, you feel you are in charge of the club. The club becomes your baby. And, you will take care of your baby no matter what. When the students had this ownership mindset, you accomplished it!


## *Set a goal*

Setting a goal is really really really important. Be it to advance to the semifinal, to the national final or to get on to the podium. It is super important. Because, as soon as we have such a goal, we actually start to work on getting there. At least, that’s how it worked for us.

I know a lot of runners. How do they train to be able to run a full marathon? They would first register a full marathon. Usually these marathons would be a few months away. And then, they will find a marathon training schedule, such as AMG 55. It’s an 18 week plan with 55 miles per week. Most of them would be able to follow the plan to 90%, and were able to finish their first marathon in their lives with a very good time score.

Why were these runners able to do that? Because as soon as they registered for the marathon event, they set themselves a clear goal. Then, they are measured on that goal. Then, a lot of them would start to think about how to prepare their bodies so that they can run a full marathon successfully. Without the registration, they won’t have such a goal and they won’t start to work on it.

Same thing for CyberPatriot, we always had a long term goal to advance to the national final. And, the short term goal for our first year was to advance to the platinum semifinal. When you set up a goal, magic happens. First, we dare to dream and we dare to pursue our dream. Now, it is worth the effort we put in to prepare for the competition. We can tell the parents that our goal is to be the top 12 teams in the nation. Compared to that, do you still want your kids to spend endless hours on a science bowl team or a robotics team and just to advance to state or region? We started to seriously figure out what it takes to get to the national final. Then, we hold ourselves accountable. We have an internal scorecard. We will review our efforts, our results against the goal we set for ourselves. 

Be declarative! A few years ago, when we first started, we told people our ultimate goal was to advance to the CyberPatriot National Finals from the Open Division. People almost laughed at us. They told us Oregon never had a team who advanced to the National Finals at all. Also, as matter of fact, Oregon didn’t even have a team who advanced to the Semifinals in Open Division before we started. We were even told that we need to accept that Oregon ranks around 47 out of 50 states in the US in terms of public education. But, we were daydreamers. We were stubborn. We put in a lot of effort. We even continued our weekly meetings during the winter break when we were in Kanab, Utah touring the beautiful orange colored national parks. We were really sad when we did not achieve what we were aiming for.

But, because we set a goal, because we were so declarative, we all know our goal, we really hold ourselves accountable. I even told my team that I won’t shave my beard until we make it to the CyberPatriot National Finals. Because of all these, students never complain that there is too much to study. We all know what it takes to get to the nats. We all feel the effort we put in is not wasted.

There is a Chinese joke, “自己吹的牛，含着泪也得实现” (You boasted it, you get it even with tears in eyes)

**Setting a goal or dare to dream is the most important thing**. Naysayers will laugh at you. People will define you guys as daydreamers. But, my greatest fear is regret. I fear that I will regret not putting in enough effort. I never see anyone regretting that they made too much effort but failed. Even if they fail, they are always proud of themselves.


## *You need a leader*

People always misunderstand leaders or leadership, by a lot. When we ask what leadership is, people may say leadership is to influence other people or to communicate or story telling when they are on the stage. And, when people are not on the stage, leadership for a lot of people is just the title. 

Leaders or leadership is none of these.

The majority of us actually witnessed leaders and leadership everyday, we just don’t realize it. Our parents are the real leaders of the family. What do they do? Like what Gus in Breaking Bad said, “provides”. Our parents provide food, shelter, clothes, safety, education, etc. We may be embarrassed by them. We may not be talking to them everyday. They might be shambles by our definition. But, they are the real leaders. They changed our diapers at 3am when we were shitting babies. They make sure the ends meet. They give the greatest hope to their children even when no one else believes them. 

Whatever the organization, whatever the goal. Real leaders are those who can bring the team through the hardest part of the journey. Hardest means when people feel hopeless. Hardest means nobody believes us. Hardest means the odds are not on our side. Hardest means when the babies are at their most vulnerable stage of their lives. Leaders don’t need to have a title. Leaders carry the team.

For CyberPatriot teams, the leader could be the coach, could be a mentor, could be the captain. Or, maybe the leader is just someone in the team without any title. This someone sees the problems of the team. This someone sees the challenges. This someone never complains. This someone worked his/her ass off just to make sure everyone on the team is really locked in and grinding. This someone tries his/her best to figure out how competition works and make a plan to tackle it. If you see you have a leader in your team, congratulations! If not, step up, carry the team, motivate the team, find necessary resources for the team, be the leader yourself. Your goal is very simple, make sure the team as an engine is ignited and running efficiently.

A lot of people complain that their team has this problem and that problem. They envy other successful teams. They think their own team has no hope. But, the reality is every team, big or small, established or unestablished, successful or not successful yet, will always encounter lots of problems. Our teams have a lot of problems, too. But, I always tell myself, *“******, if we have so many problems, there is no way other successful teams don’t have the same problems. It’s just that they can solve and overcome these problems.”* If we have no learning materials, figure out a way to find learning materials. If other people can do it, we can do it. If we don’t have money for laptops, figure out a way to get them. If people are not locked in, figure out a way to motivate them to lock in or to find who are really motivated. Trust me, every team has a lot of problems. Non-leaders will complain about those problems and give themselves all kinds of excuses for not being successful; leaders will face those problems head on and try their best to solve those problems. 

Leaders take the blame, leaders make no excuses, leaders solve the problems!


## *Know your team*

I personally love to watch sports, all kinds of sports. And, I noticed that there are typically 2 types of coaches, one I call tactics coaches,  such as  Pep Guardiola, Jürgen Klopp, etc.. Pep is the frontier of new tactics, strategies and theory in the soccer world. The other one I call people coaches, such as Carlo Anchelotti, Zinedine Zidane, etc.. These coaches may not seem to have the new tactics or new theory. But, they can build the chemistry in a team, they know each player really well. They know how to motivate people, even if they are big stars like CR7. And, if you take a look at those successful soccer coaches, you will see that they usually speak multiple languages, which hints about the importance of communication. Even Pep and Klopp are people coaches themselves.

A team is always made up of people, in our case, the students. And, the hardest part for every organization is always its people. And, the most valuable asset of every organization is always its people. If we don’t feel the value of our people, we are underusing them and/or abusing them. As a coach, we need to get to know every student as much as we can. We need to know what’s in their mind. We need to know what’s important to each of them. We need to know how to motivate them the most.

For me, my first priority is always to figure out what problems the students have. And, whether they are competition related or not, if they are hurdles to your students, do your best to help them to figure out how to solve these problems. Sometimes the problems are way beyond our capability to solve them. At least, we understand the existence of these problems and live with them. Surprisingly, when those problems are solved, you will see students’ improvement.

Also, as an unc, I try my best to speak the students’ language. I understand what they brag about. I understand what’s important for them. Then, I started to know how to motivate them. People are different. For some of them, you give them a leaderboard with scores, they will grind just for these scores. For some of them, you lay out a big picture, tell them what to do and why it works, they will understand it and will get it done three months later. For some of them, you give them a deadline, and they will get it done. 

The best result I got so far is when I can individually talk with the students about their goal, their plan and their execution. And, when they start to give me updates and discuss different choices with me, I know they are locked in and we can expect great results from them.

My best student once asked me what were my expectations for him, and did he meet my expectations. I told him, *“No, I have no expectations for you at all. It’s your life. You own the scorecard of your own life. You tell yourself whether you did a good job or not. You make the decisions of your own life. And, you take responsibility for the consequences of your decisions.”* You treat your students as a grown up, they will grow up.


## *Try something new, always*

When you have been doing something for many years, it is very easy to fall into a situation where you feel it’s all the same. It is not. You can never sleep on your previous success. In this world, you are always padding a boat against the stream. Once you stop padding, you are destined to fail. This is a trap.

My fight against this trap is I always try to do something new. And, I let students know that they need to do something new every season. You are already pretty good on Cisco, why not get a CCNA this year. You are good at CTFs, why not create some CTF challenges yourself? If you understand AD DC so well, create a small group of machines that are using it. We have new students coming to the club, how to motivate them and catch up to the level. If a small competition motivates students, let’s do it, let’s create a skirmish.

Trying something new is not natural in most people’s genes. Evolution gave us the capability to try something new. But, only when the old way does not work anymore. Evolution gave us the capability to survive not to win. In order to win, we need to constantly look for something new. Every year, I myself try something else to motivate students. I myself look for new stuff to learn, new resources for the students. That makes me really excited. So, I also try my best to convey this message to my team. We try new stuff to do team building. Over the years, we played bowling, we played race cars, we played archery, we played soccer golf, we played airsoft and I injured my best student, we played curling, we played bridge, we played poker games.

I figured if we really need to be at front, we need to always explore new stuff, period.


## *To share*

The first time I encountered sharing is from the First LEGO League core values, I really liked its old core values where it stated “We share our experiences with others”. And, over the years, I became a true believer of sharing.

Initially, Coach Pei and I didn’t really understand why we needed to share our knowledge, even to our competitors. And, we couldn’t really convince those students and parents who doubted the sharing. But, nevertheless, we rigorously followed FLL core values, We shared everything to everyone, including our opponents and competitors. Surprisingly, when we start to share, we see a lot of people and teams who are willing to share as well. Most of these sharing teams are top teams. I figured these teams are learning from each other. And, all these sharing teams are focusing more on learning new things rather guarding whatever they have. When they try to share, they try their best to polish the materials they want to share. So, they learned way more by just trying to share. These teams are proud of what they are doing. This itself gives the teams a lot more motivation. Those teams form a league, form a forum, they lead the progress.

And, it is true in a lot of places. In basketball world, you need to goto NBA to be the best. In soccer, you need to goto Europe top leagues. In the academia world, you need to participate in these conferences and share your knowledge to be the best.

It’s the same thing for CyberPatriot. A few years ago, there were very few learning materials around for CyberPatriot. Now, there are a decent amount of community practice images and learning materials around. It is not the effort of a single team. It is the effort of all these sharing teams. And, surprisingly, most of these practice images are created by those teams who advanced to the National Finals. I guess you will learn a lot when trying to build your masterpiece of practice images. You try your best to polish it so that it is worth a spot in the Louvre. And, our team built a Louvre, we try our best to host all these images in our museum.

As Mentor Sean quoted, *“be the change you want to see in the world”*. We did it. 


## *To win*

The GOAT in the female pingpong world Deng Yaping once said, “To win, you just need to really really want to win.”

I don’t know if we have that mindset. But, I always tell my team that whatever you do, make sure 10 years or 20 years later you don’t regret what you did today. I have seen too many could have should have, might have, but have not in my life. I told my students, I just want you to be proud of what you did today. 20 or 40 years from today, when you talk to your kids, when you talk to your grandkids, you show them your dusty trophy or your photos to tell them you won it when you are in high school. I guess that might really motivate them. Or, maybe they were motivated all by themselves.

Yes, Pete Carroll said competition happens during everyday practice, not just on game days. But, one thing I want to say is the reason why a competition is called competition is because until the competition is over, the champion is not decided, yet. Yes, in the book Team A might be the best. They are so advanced. They are way beyond us. But, we still need to give 100% of ourselves. We don’t give ourselves any excuse. When we competed in the National Finals, we brought physical Cisco Routers and Switches all the way from Oregon to the hotel in DC. (Don’t get me wrong, those are old devices we got from eBay for 50 bucks each.) Yes, TSA confiscated our crimping tools. We bought another set from Amazon immediately and got it delivered to the hotel in DC the next day. We practice crimping the CAT crystal connector in the hotel room until minutes before the competition. Yes, the server in our garage is down when we are in DC. We called and Facetimed one of the non-tech savvy parents to go to our house and turn the server On. People always talk about how you fight against being nervous, our answer is you do it by focusing on detailed preparation.

During the season CPXV, we explicitly declared that our goal for that season for team 1 was to advance to the National Final. And, the students worked really hard. And, our CCS score in semifinal was really good, we were No. 8. Because 12 teams would advance to the National Final, even if we did not do well in Cisco, we have a decent safety margin. But, when the Cisco score came out, we missed the National Final by a very small number. We were so devastated that season. It was really painful. And, the next season, the entire team learned Cisco. We all learned OSPF, VPN, ACL, AAA. And, we finally advanced to the National Final. I guess sometimes the experience of losing when you worked really hard can genuinely motivate people.

No team can guarantee to win every year. But, some teams do their best every year to work on it and leave no regret. And, maybe someday, Lady Luck will smile upon those teams.


## *Prepare for the change*

>*"Change is like rain during a soccer game. It falls on you and your opponent equally. If the conditions suck, they suck for both sides. But if you’re mentally and physically prepared to adapt while your opponent isn’t, it becomes your edge." –Sun Tzu*

To be honest, I love changes. I know when there are changes, a lot of other teams will be freaked out or be totally frustrated. And, I tell my teams that we don’t complain about our environment. It is beyond our control. We focus on adapting to the changes and surviving.

As a matter of fact, all winning teams have that mentality. One year, CyberPatriot put a Fedora, a completely new Linux image, in the semifinal competition. CyberAegis team 1 scored 62 out 100 when the rest teams scored an average of 3 (including us). Clearly, they were much much better prepared for the change than any other team.

Every year, there will be some changes with CyberPatriot competition, bigger or smaller, better or worse. Winners will adapt to it and losers will whine about it. This is actually really true even in the rest of the real world. Changes happen all the time. It’s your call to whine or to win.


## *We are a team*

At the beginning of the CyberPatriot competition, we did not feel too much teamwork was needed. Everyone is working on their own virtual machine competition. However, the more we competed, the more we felt that teamwork is everywhere. Our students take turns to teach the club on different topics. Also, the top students create team assignment tests for the rest of the club. And, during the competition, you will clearly see that the best team is always very good at communications. They always tell their teammates about what they did, what’s working, what’s not, what needs to be done. Plus, in the National Final, the team have to fight against the red team collaboratively. A team is not built in one day. We share the sad days together. We share happiness together. We work together, play together. We know who is good at what, who is bad at what. Our students spent more time together than with their parents. Over the years, because we know each other and we trust each other, we can stand back-to-back confidently and face our opponents together. 

Every time we compete in DC, I tell my students to reach out to other students, to reach out to the organizers and sponsors. Today, they are competing against us, but we are friends. Tomorrow, they will be our classmates at college, they will be our coworkers. They will be part of our network for the rest of our lives. Competition won’t last forever. But, the network can.

In a sense, we all are a team.
